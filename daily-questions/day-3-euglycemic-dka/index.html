Euglycemic DKA with an SGLT2 Inhibitor

A 54-year-old patient with type 2 diabetes takes empagliflozin and basal insulin. After 2 days of vomiting and markedly reduced oral intake, the patient reports abdominal pain, nausea, and rapid breathing.

Labs

Glucose
178 mg/dL
Bicarbonate
14 mEq/L
Anion gap
22
Serum beta-hydroxybutyrate
4.1 mmol/L
Venous pH
7.28

What is the most likely diagnosis and next step?

  1. ADKA is unlikely because the glucose is below 250 mg/dL
  2. BEuglycemic DKA; arrange urgent emergency evaluation and hold the SGLT2 inhibitor
  3. CHyperosmolar hyperglycemic state; encourage oral hydration at home
  4. DSimple viral gastroenteritis; continue all diabetes medications

ANSWER + CLINICAL REASONING

Correct answer: B

The patient has high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis and significant ketosis despite a glucose of only 178 mg/dL.

SGLT2 inhibitors such as empagliflozin can cause euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis. Important findings include an elevated anion gap, low bicarbonate, acidemia, and elevated beta-hydroxybutyrate.

The relatively normal glucose does not exclude DKA. Vomiting and reduced oral intake can precipitate euglycemic DKA in a patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor.

Clinical Pearl

Consider euglycemic DKA in patients taking an SGLT2 inhibitor who develop nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, or tachypnea even when glucose is below 250 mg/dL. Urgent emergency evaluation is appropriate.
