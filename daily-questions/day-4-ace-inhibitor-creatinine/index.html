ACE Inhibitor Creatinine Increase in Diabetic CKD

A 58-year-old patient with type 2 diabetes and hypertension presents for follow-up.

Medications: metformin and amlodipine 10 mg daily.

Initial findings: BP 148/88 mmHg, creatinine 1.0 mg/dL, potassium 4.3 mEq/L, eGFR 78 mL/min/1.73 m², and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio 420 mg/g.

Because of persistent hypertension and significant albuminuria, lisinopril is started.

Two weeks later: BP 136/80 mmHg, creatinine 1.25 mg/dL, potassium 4.8 mEq/L, and the patient feels well.

What is the most appropriate next step?

  1. AStop lisinopril immediately because creatinine increased
  2. BContinue lisinopril and monitor renal function and potassium
  3. CStop lisinopril and increase amlodipine
  4. DRefer the patient urgently to the emergency department
